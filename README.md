# Hands_On_Quantum_Education

Learning outcomes: Labs 1–8  

Lab 1 | Introduction to polarization and qubit encoding
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 1.1 — Demonstrate how a half-wave plate controls the polarization state of laser light to encode binary qubit states by routing light to either the horizontal (|0⟩) or vertical (|1⟩) output port of a polarizing beamsplitter.
LO 1.2 — Observe the equal superposition state (|0⟩ + |1⟩) by rotating the half-wave plate to 22.5° and confirming an approximately equal power split between the two output ports.
LO 1.3 — Apply qubit encoding principles in a practical communication exercise by using polarization states to transmit and decode a sequence of binary signals.

Lab 2 | Superposition with half-wave plate
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 2.1 — Demonstrate how a half-wave plate alters the polarization of a laser beam and measure the resulting change in light intensity.
LO 2.2 — Verify Malus’s law by comparing predicted and measured power output at various half-wave plate angles.
LO 2.3 — Explore the concept of quantum superposition by observing how polarization state determines photon path through a polarized beamsplitter.

Lab 3 | Measurement and basis selection
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 3.1 — Demonstrate how polarization basis selection using a half-wave plate can encode binary information into light, forming the foundation of a quantum key distribution system.
LO 3.2 — Simulate secure quantum communication by comparing sender and receiver basis selections and identifying which shared measurements constitute a valid cryptographic key.
LO 3.3 — Illustrate how eavesdropping on a quantum channel is detectable by observing how an interceptor’s random basis selections reduce the sender–receiver match rate from 50% to 25%.

Lab 4 | Quarter-wave plate and circular polarization
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 4.1 — Demonstrate how a quarter-wave plate at 45° converts linearly polarized light into circularly polarized light and observe the resulting 50/50 power split at the polarizing beamsplitter outputs.
LO 4.2 — Illustrate measurement basis incompatibility by showing that a linear polarizing beamsplitter cannot distinguish circular polarization states without first converting them back to linear polarization using a second quarter-wave plate.
LO 4.3 — Connect the behavior of circularly polarized light at a beamsplitter to the quantum mechanical principle that measuring a qubit in an incompatible basis yields probabilistic rather than deterministic outcomes.

Lab 5 | Hadamard gate implementation
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 5.1 — Demonstrate how a half-wave plate rotated to 22.5° physically implements the Hadamard gate by placing polarized light into an equal superposition of horizontal and vertical states.
LO 5.2 — Verify the predicted 50/50 power split at the polarizing beamsplitter outputs using Malus’s law across multiple half-wave plate angles.
LO 5.3 — Explore the self-inverse property of the Hadamard gate by observing how two successive half-wave plates at 22.5° return light to its original polarization state.

Lab 6 | NOT gate with half-wave plate
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 6.1 — Demonstrate how a half-wave plate rotated to 45° functions as a quantum NOT gate by fully inverting the polarization state of incoming light from horizontal to vertical and vice versa.
LO 6.2 — Verify the self-canceling property of the NOT gate by placing two half-wave plates in series and confirming the output polarization matches the original input.
LO 6.3 — Investigate how varying the half-wave plate angle affects the distribution of power between the horizontal and vertical outputs of a polarizing beamsplitter.

Lab 7 | Phase shift with wave plates
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 7.1 — Measure how a half-wave plate introduces a phase shift between polarization components and verify that the resulting transmitted intensity follows the relationship I = I₀ cos²(2θ).
LO 7.2 — Identify the wave plate angles corresponding to maximum and minimum light transmission and connect those observations to the plate’s effect on the polarization direction of the output beam.
LO 7.3 — Compare predicted and measured power values across a range of half-wave plate angles to evaluate the accuracy of Malus’s law as a model for this optical system.

Lab 8 | Sequential gate operations
Upon completion of this lab, learners should be able to:
LO 8.1 — Demonstrate that quantum gate operations are order-dependent by comparing the power distribution at the beamsplitter outputs for the H→X and X→H gate sequences.
LO 8.2 — Quantify the probability of measuring each polarization state (|0⟩ and |1⟩) after each two-gate circuit and connect those probabilities to the sequential effect of each half-wave plate on the light’s polarization.
LO 8.3 — Verify the self-inverse properties of the Hadamard and NOT gates by confirming that applying each gate twice in succession returns the light to its original horizontal polarization state.
